# Workshop Timer Strategy

## Target problem

A solo facilitator delivering a multi-hour workshop must present content, read the room, respond to participants, and monitor questions simultaneously. Timekeeping competes for the same limited attention, making it easy for individual segments—and eventually the entire workshop—to run over.

## Our approach

Move timing decisions into session preparation by distributing the workshop across planned segments. During delivery, provide glanceable awareness and timely nudges so the facilitator can stay present, adapt before small slips become larger problems, and avoid continuously watching the clock.

## Who it's for

**Primary:** Solo facilitators delivering multi-hour workshops — They’re hiring Workshop Timer to act as an unobtrusive timekeeper so they can focus on the participants and content while keeping the session on schedule.

## Key metrics

- **Manual clock checks** — How often the facilitator feels compelled to check the time independently during a workshop; captured through post-workshop reflection.
- **Nudge confidence** — Whether the facilitator trusts that nudges arrive early enough to make a useful adjustment; captured through post-workshop reflection.
- **Segment-time adherence** — The difference between the planned and actual duration of each workshop segment.
- **Workshop finish adherence** — The difference between the scheduled and actual end time of the complete workshop.

## Tracks

### Session preparation

Plan the workshop as a sequence of segments and distribute the available time across them.

_Why it serves the approach:_ It moves timing decisions out of the live workshop, when the facilitator’s attention is already stretched.

### Live guidance

Maintain lightweight awareness of the current segment and provide nudges at moments when the facilitator can still act.

_Why it serves the approach:_ It keeps the facilitator informed without requiring continual clock-watching.

### Schedule recovery

Help the facilitator make deliberate adjustments when a segment runs over or the workshop changes course.

_Why it serves the approach:_ It prevents a local timing change from silently pushing the entire workshop off schedule.

### Workshop reflection

Compare the planned allocation with how time was actually distributed.

_Why it serves the approach:_ It turns each workshop into evidence for planning the next one more realistically.
